# J0 — THE SMOOTH TABLE (frame rate)

Branch `claude/j0-smooth-table`, rebased on main `b9720b6` (2.0 with J1/J2/J3). Only `src/core.js`
(loop, resize, render passes) and `probes/` changed. No gameplay number, no save key, no gauge code touched.

## What it feels like
- **A slow machine no longer plays in slow motion.** Before, any frame slower than 50 ms made the whole
  night crawl (at 10 fps the world ran at half speed: the "1 second in game is 5 seconds real" feeling).
  Now a long frame is simulated as up to two half-steps, so the clock, the enemies and the music stay in
  step with real time down to 10 fps. Below 10 fps it still slows (on purpose: it stops a hitch from
  teleporting the horde onto you).
- **One heartbeat.** rAF now owns the frame. The 15 ms backup ticker only takes over when the tab is
  hidden or rAF has gone quiet for 100 ms (embedded previews still run). Before, the two raced each other,
  which made the frame pacing uneven.
- **Much less paint per frame.** About 45% fewer full screens painted per frame in a 300-enemy storm. The
  look is the same. The sigil's engraved rings are drawn from a half-res copy (they were half-res sprites
  already), and the fog and vignette are stacked once into a half-res layer (they are soft gradients).
- **The low tier is real now.** When the House "smooths the felt", it first drops to 1.5× resolution
  (not straight to 1×). If the game is still slow 5 s later, it drops to 1×. It climbs back the same two
  steps. The low tier also turns off the light shafts, freezes the sigil's slow turning, keeps one
  wandering candle pool instead of two, and drops the pillar light pools. The pillar flames keep their glow.
  Phones still boot at 1×, as before.

| `perf-ab.js` render() JS ms med / p95 | 2.5 / 5.4 | 2.5–8.5 / 10–14 |

Notes on the numbers:
- On the first base (`6100c49`) the same storm measured 24.9–26.0 → 14.1–14.3.
- Headless Chromium has no GPU, so the absolute ms are software raster. The render() p95 rises because
  the half-res sigil and atmosphere layers are drawn on the CPU there, and that work moves from the raster
  column into render(). Raster plus render together still drop by about 20–25%. On a GPU browser those
  offscreen draws stay on the GPU.
- `onCanvas` counts only the pixels that can actually land: clipped to the canvas and to rectangular clips.
  The old metric counted the sigil's off-screen 1456² layers in full even though they were clipped. This
  number is the honest one at DPR 1. The win grows at DPR 2, because the sigil and atmosphere layers are
  DPR-independent half-res canvases.

## What changed, step by step
1. **One frame driver** (`loop`/`tick`): rAF drives. The interval only drives when `document.hidden` or
   rAF has been silent for 100 ms. The 8 ms dedup stays as a belt.
2. **Real time**: the `dt` cap went from 0.05 to 0.1. A frame longer than 50 ms is simulated in two equal
   slices. The `update(dt*1.71*(slowT>0?0.35:1))` semantics are kept per slice, and `hitstop` is spent per
   slice. A slice that changes state (slot opens, death) ends the frame's simulation. wheel/crash/slot
   ticks get the same slicing.
3. **Overlay bake.** I did not do it the way the brief said. `warm` is drawn with alpha `0.5+excite*0.5`,
   `main` with the candle flicker `cf`, and `grd` with the `overlay` blend, so none of them is static. I
   tried folding `grd` into `main` by fitting a single source-over layer per pixel. It lost the grade's
   centre brightening (up to ~14/255 on mid-tones near the player, visible in a diff), so I backed it out.
   Instead **fog + danger + main are stacked into one half-res "atmosphere" canvas** (`atmosDraw`). All
   three are plain source-over, and source-over is associative, so the stack is the same picture. It is
   re-laid every 6th frame or at once on a rebake. A pixel diff against the direct draws is at the noise
   floor. Fog now sits above embers, grain and warm instead of below. That is second-order: fog alpha is
   ≤0.07. `warm` (screen) and `grd` (overlay) stay their own passes.
   Also **grain** is one pattern `fillRect` of exactly the screen (it was ~62 tile blits spilling past the
   edges), and **floor edge tiles** blit only their on-screen part.
4. **Sigil**: the four layers are composed into one 728² half-res canvas (`sigilCompose`). Only the patch
   the camera sees (+64 px) is composed, and each layer draws only the source rect that can land in the
   patch at its angle. It is recomposed when the fastest ring has turned about 1 px (`Δ>0.05` game-s,
   about every 2nd frame) or the camera leaves the patch. Then the visible part is blitted once with
   smoothing. The off-camera cull stays. Diff vs the old draw: ≤1/255 along the engraved lines.
   The sigil keeps its own clock (`_sigT`), equal to `gt` on high, so the rings stop on the low tier
   and resume without a jump.
5. **Half-res light buffer: not done, on purpose.** The `lightSprite` draws (two candle pools plus the
   halo) paint about 0.63 screens per frame and barely overlap. A buffer has to be composited across their
   union, about 0.65 screens at full device resolution, plus its own fill. That is a net loss in both
   metrics, and worse at DPR 2. It would also re-order what gets lit (coins, orbs, the new FEED halo). The
   FEED halo is untouched.
6. **Low tier**: see above. `resize()` caps DPR at 1.5 on the first trip (`_qDeep===false`) and at 1 when
   deep (forced `low`, phone boot, or still slow after the 5 s dwell). The thresholds (`frameAvg>19`,
   `<15.5`×45, 5 s dwell, 30-frame grace) are unchanged.
7. **Other passes**: the grain and floor-edge fixes above. The full-screen clear stays. Without it, tile
   seams at fractional positions would show last frame's pixels.

## Probes
- `probes/pace.js` (new): steps 16.7/60/100/150 ms frames for 10 s each. It compares `gt` against real
  time × 1.71, skipping frames touched by hit-stop, slow-mo, TAB or a gambling screen. It exits 1 if
  60 ms or 100 ms is below 95%.
- `probes/fillarea.js`: adds `screensPerFrame_total` and a clip-aware `screensPerFrame_onCanvas`. It also
  takes optional args 6 `SEED` and 7 `heart`. `heart` parks the storm on the sigil, the worst case, so A/B
  runs compare the same scene. The bot's night is not fully deterministic even when seeded.

## Shots
`shots/j0/` is the after-set. I compared it by eye against a set captured the same way from main's
`b9720b6` dist (not committed: the bot's nights differ run to run, so the scenes are not identical).
- **Desktop (menu, slot, 0:30, 3:00, ledger):** same vignette depth, same warm heart and cold edges, same
  engraved sigil, fog and grain. I found no visible difference beyond the different scenes.
- **Phone play:** phones boot on the low tier (as before, still at 1×). Under the new real low tier the
  pale screen-space light shafts and the pillar light pools are gone there. This is the one visible
  change, and the brief asks for it. If the director misses the shafts on phones, revert the `!qLow`
  guard on the screen-space shafts in `render()`.

## What's left
- The remaining full-screen passes are the clear, floor, gore ledger, grain, warm (screen), atmosphere,
  grade (overlay), the hurt/heartbeat flash, and the walls. Folding `grd` needs a blend-exact trick (it
  brightens the centre multiplicatively), and I did not find one that keeps the look.
- The sigil recompose (~every 2nd frame) shows up as render() p95 on software-raster machines. If that
  matters, raise the threshold in `drawSigil` from 0.05 to 0.08. The rings would tick in ~1.3 px steps.
- Frames slower than 100 ms (below 10 fps) still slow the world. That is deliberate.
